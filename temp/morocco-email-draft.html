🌍 10 Best Places to Visit in Morocco

A curated travel guide to the most breathtaking destinations in the Kingdom of Morocco

1

Marrakech — Jemaa el-Fna Square

Jemaa el-Fna is the beating heart of Marrakech's ancient medina and a UNESCO Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity. By day it is filled with orange-juice stalls, snake charmers, and henna artists; by night it transforms into a vast open-air theatre of storytellers, musicians, and street-food vendors. The square is the gateway to the labyrinthine souks, riads, and monuments of one of Africa's most iconic cities.

2

Chefchaouen — The Blue City

Chefchaouen is a small mountain city in the Rif range of northwestern Morocco, world-famous for its striking medina of blue-washed walls, stairways, and alleyways. Founded in 1471, the blue tones are said to have been introduced by Jewish refugees in the 1930s and have become the city's defining identity. It is one of Morocco's most photogenic destinations and a popular base for hiking into the surrounding forested mountains.

3

Fes — Fes el-Bali Medina

Fes el-Bali is the oldest and largest medieval urban centre in the world, a UNESCO World Heritage Site founded in the 9th century. Its dense network of over 9,000 narrow streets contains mosques, madrasas, fondouks, and the world's oldest continuously operating university, the University of al-Qarawiyyin. The Chouara tannery, where leather has been dyed in stone vats using ancient methods for centuries, is among its most iconic sights.

4

Sahara Desert — Erg Chebbi Dunes near Merzouga

Erg Chebbi is Morocco's most spectacular sand sea, with dunes rising up to 150 metres on the far western fringe of the Sahara Desert near the small village of Merzouga. The dunes glow in shades of orange, gold, and deep red, especially at sunrise and sunset, and camel treks into the desert with overnight stays in Berber camps are the signature experience. It is one of only two true ergs in Morocco and draws visitors from around the world.

5

Essaouira — The Windy City

Essaouira is a windswept Atlantic port city with a beautifully preserved 18th-century medina enclosed by Portuguese-style ramparts, also a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its blue-and-white fishing harbour, art galleries, and laid-back creative atmosphere have made it a favourite among artists, musicians, and travellers for decades. The city is also one of Africa's top windsurfing and kitesurfing destinations thanks to the relentless trade winds known locally as the "alizé".

6

Aït Benhaddou — The Fortified Kasbah

Aït Benhaddou is a breathtaking fortified ksar (mud-brick village) along the former caravan route between the Sahara and Marrakech, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987. Its towering earthen kasbahs, granaries, and communal towers are considered among the finest examples of southern Moroccan earthen architecture. The site has been used as a filming location for dozens of major productions including Gladiator, Game of Thrones, and Lawrence of Arabia.

7

Casablanca — Hassan II Mosque

The Hassan II Mosque in Casablanca is the largest mosque in Africa and the 14th largest in the world, completed in 1993. Its minaret, at 210 metres, is the world's second tallest, and a laser beam at its tip points toward Mecca each night. Uniquely, part of the mosque is built over the Atlantic Ocean, incorporating the Quranic verse that "the throne of God was built on water" into its very foundations.

8

Atlas Mountains — Roof of North Africa

The Atlas Mountains stretch 2,500 kilometres across Morocco, Algeria, and Tunisia, with the High Atlas in Morocco containing the highest peak in North Africa, Jbel Toubkal at 4,167 metres. The range divides the Sahara from the fertile coastal plains and is home to Amazigh (Berber) villages, cedar forests, cascading waterfalls, and dramatic gorges. Trekking, skiing at Oukaïmeden, and mule rides through remote valleys are among the many activities that draw adventurers here year-round.

9

Ouarzazate — Gateway to the Sahara

Ouarzazate, often called the "Gateway to the Sahara", sits at the crossroads of the High Atlas and the pre-Saharan desert valleys of the Draa and Dades rivers. It is home to the stunning Kasbah Taourirt and to the world's largest film studios by area, Atlas Corporation Studios. Films from Lawrence of Arabia to Gladiator and Game of Thrones were shot in and around this dramatically beautiful desert city.

10

Rabat — Morocco's Capital

Rabat, Morocco's capital city, blends a UNESCO-listed historic core with a modern administrative centre on the banks of the Bou Regreg river. Its most iconic landmark is the Hassan Tower, the unfinished 12th-century minaret of what would have been the world's largest mosque, standing amid a field of broken columns beside the ornate Mausoleum of Mohammed V. The city's Kasbah of the Udayas, ancient Roman ruins at Chellah, and French colonial boulevard architecture round out an often-overlooked but richly layered destination.
