# My Take: Pharma's AI Problem Isn't Discovery — It's Development

Here's my take. AI is compressing drug discovery into months. But clinical development — the trials, the regulatory gauntlet, getting a drug from lab to patient — is moving in the opposite direction. Timelines have grown by a third. R&D spending keeps climbing while productivity stays flat. Pharma built a supersonic jet and left it idling on the runway.

The industry is aware. Companies are running AI pilots everywhere. But only 28 percent ever reach production. The rest is innovation theater. What pharma needs is not more pilots — it's orchestration.

The biggest prize is protocol design. Today, the scientific, operational, and competitive dimensions of a trial are tackled in sequence and in silos. AI can collapse them, using digital twins and simulations to assess feasibility, cost, and timelines simultaneously. Only 13 percent of companies are piloting this, but those who are expect a fourfold ROI. Shaving 20 to 30 percent off development timelines translates to hundreds of millions in earlier revenue per drug — billions at the portfolio level.

Yet the future is not the "autonomous trial." Bain's research shows a better investigator experience leads to faster recruitment and completion. Past transformations in pharma — remote monitoring, risk-based oversight — failed when technology leapfrogged trust. The companies pulling ahead are co-creating tools with site staff and investigators, proving value through measurable efforts before demanding adoption.

The practical path forward: pick two or three high-value workflows, chase usable data over perfect data, build orchestrated AI systems with humans in the loop, and make a clear strategic choice about where to win first. The cost of inaction isn't just financial. It's patients waiting for treatments that already exist in a lab but haven't yet survived clinical development.

And that's my take.
